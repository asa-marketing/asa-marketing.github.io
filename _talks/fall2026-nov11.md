# Learning When to Quit in Sales Conversations

Salespeople frequently face the dynamic qualification decision of whether to persist in a conversation or abandon it to pursue the next lead. Yet, little is known about whether these decisions are efficient or how firms can improve them. We study high-volume outbound sales, where leads are ample, time is scarce, and failure is common. We formalize dynamic qualification as an optimal stopping problem and develop a large language model (LLM)-based sequential decision agent — a stopping agent — that learns when to quit from historical conversations via offline reinforcement learning. When applied to calls from a large European telecommunications firm, our stopping agent reduces total call time by 67% while retaining 83% of observed sales. If the time saved is reallocated to new calls drawn from the same distribution, expected sales increase by up to 47%. Upon examining the linguistic cues associated with salespeople’s quitting decisions, we find that their decisions are disproportionately associated with a few salient expressions of prospect disinterest and appear to overlook early indicators of eventual failure. These patterns are consistent with cognitive bounds on salespeople’s ability to integrate and act on conversational information in real time. Our findings illustrate how offline reinforcement learning can be deployed to harness the natural language understanding capabilities of LLMs to improve the allocation of scarce salesperson time.
