Estimation Bias from Machine-Learned Features in Econometric Models

Nikhil Malik
Tepper School of Business, Carnegie Mellon University
Join via Zoom ↗
Abstract

This paper examines a popular practice where we estimate an econometric model that relates an outcome of interest (e.g., rental bookings) to a focal feature in unstructured data (e.g., pet presence in property photos). The focal feature is often extracted by a machine learning model, which introduces extraction errors. We delineate several different ways that the extraction errors may introduce bias in the estimate of the econometric model. Importantly, these errors may contain non-focal, or “peripheral,” features of the unstructured data that have effects on the outcome of interest. We describe a way to correct the bias caused by extraction errors, accounting for these effects of the peripheral features. The method is applicable to general, nonlinear econometric models. We provide consistency results for the method and show that it performs well across a range of Monte Carlo experiments and in an empirical application.
